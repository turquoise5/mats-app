Given:

$$
f(x) = \frac{x^2}{x + 1}
$$

### Step 1: Recall the quotient rule

The quotient rule states that if:

$$
f(x) = \frac{u(x)}{v(x)}
$$

then:

$$
f'(x) = \frac{u'(x)v(x) - u(x)v'(x)}{[v(x)]^2}
$$

### Step 2: Identify $ u(x) $ and $ v(x) $

Let:

- $ u(x) = x^2 $
- $ v(x) = x + 1 $

### Step 3: Compute derivatives

- $ u'(x) = 2x $
- $ v'(x) = 1 $

### Step 4: Apply the quotient rule

$$
f'(x) = \frac{u'(x)v(x) - u(x)v'(x)}{[v(x)]^2}
= \frac{(2x)(x + 1) - (x^2)(1)}{(x + 1)^2}
$$

### Step 5: Simplify the numerator

$$
(2x)(x + 1) = 2x^2 + 2x
$$
$$
(x^2)(1) = x^2
$$
$$
\text{Numerator} = 2x^2 + 2x - x^2 = x^2 + 2x
$$

### Final Answer:

$$
f'(x) = \frac{x^2 + 2x}{(x + 1)^2}
$$

### So, your original expression:

$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x(x + 1) - x^2}{(x + 1)}
$$

is **not fully simplified**. It is **correct in form**, but it can be simplified further to:

$$
f'(x) = \frac{x^2 + 2x}{(x + 1)^2}
$$
