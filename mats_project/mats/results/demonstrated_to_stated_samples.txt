### 📌 The Quadratic Formula

For a quadratic equation in the form:

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0
$$

The **quadratic formula** is:

$$
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

Where:
- $ a $ is the coefficient of $ x^2 $
- $ b $ is the coefficient of $ x $
- $ c $ is the constant term

---

### ✅ Example Problem

Let’s work through this equation together:

$$
2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0
$$

Here:
- $ a = 2 $
- $ b = 5 $
- $ c = -3 $

Now plug these into the quadratic formula:

$$
x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{5^2 - 4(2)(-3)}}{2(2)}
$$

Let’s simplify step by step:

1. **Calculate the discriminant** (the part under the square root):

$$
b^2 - 4ac = 5^2 - 4(2)(-3) = 25 - (-24) = 25 + 24 = 49
$$

2. **Plug back into the formula**:

$$
x = \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{49}}{4}
$$

3. **Simplify the square root**:

$$
\sqrt{49} = 7
$$

4. **Now solve for both values of $ x $**:

$$
x = \frac{-5 + 7}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
x = \frac{-5 - 7}{4} = \frac{-12}{4} = -3
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:

$$
x = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad x = -3
$$

---

### 🧠 Summary

- The **quadratic formula** works even when $ a \neq 1 $.
- Just plug in the values of $ a $, $ b $, and $ c $ into the formula.
- Be careful with signs (especially when $ c $ is negative).
- Always simplify the discriminant first.
